# How I went from 195 to 700 followers on X in 4 days

On Friday 25 September 2026 at 6:41 in the morning (Brussels time), my X account had 195 followers.

By Tuesday it had around 700.

That account had been stuck for years. For 2 to 3 years I was barely active, because I was dealing with depression and a sleep apnea that nobody had diagnosed yet. So this is not a big account getting bigger. It's a dead account coming back.

Here is how the count moved:

| When | Followers |
|---|---|
| Fri 06:41 | 195 |
| Sat 04:19 | 227 |
| Sat 14:31 | 308 |
| Sat 17:58 | 352 |
| Sat 22:48 | 407 |
| Sun ~07:45 (49 hours in) | 505 |
| Sun 18:05 | 605 |
| Sun 21:05 (62 hours in) | 621 |
| Mon 17:20 | 669 |
| Tue | ~700 |

I didn't invent the method. I copied it from Rob Hallam, who ran a public growth experiment a few days before. I pulled his posts apart, found what actually worked, and then did it myself. On Sunday, Rob (@robj3d3) quote-tweeted my pinned intro post, which was a nice moment.

I said I would open source the full step-by-step playbook, so here it is. The kit has three things:

1. This playbook.
2. The zero-to-1k finder and its Chrome extension, which find rising posts for you to reply under. The README explains how to set it up.
3. The research on Rob's run: the numbers and tables I used to build the rules below.

In this playbook I am going to show you what Rob did, the one insight that explains most of it, and then the six steps I followed, with my real numbers and the real posts.

## What Rob actually did

Rob ran his experiment on a persona account called @robmailserver. Later he revealed on his real account (@robj3d3) that the persona was fake. I want to be honest about that up front. He also admitted publicly that follow-for-follow was part of his conversations. So some of his growth came from people following each other back, and you should know that before you copy anything.

Still, the numbers were interesting. He went from about 0 to 100 followers in about 30 hours, and to about 200 in about 2 days (22 to 23 September 2026).

I pulled his full post history (638 posts) and every post he replied to during his first two days (264 replies), and I looked at what actually got views.

**Day 1 was not "let's connect".** Most people think Rob grew by posting "let's be friends" everywhere. On day 1 he didn't do that at all. He made 64 replies, 50 of them to the original poster, mostly under AI news posts (model launches, coding takes). His replies were short, funny one-liners. His own posts that day were AI news quote tweets, and they flopped at around 150 views each.

**Day 2 was the intro and the friends.** On day 2 he posted an intro that started like this: "I'm 27. Solo founder from Singapore... $1.8k MRR... say hi!" It got 3,253 views and 61 replies. After that, 72 of his 205 replies were some version of "nice to meet you, let's be X friends". And every 2 to 3 hours he posted a milestone. "100 followers in 24 hours" got 1,766 views. "2 years ago I was broke and depressed" got 1,112 views.

A small detail I noticed: 9 of his top 10 posts had an emoji, and 14 of his posts ended with ❤️.

### The key insight: a reply borrows its reach from the post under it

This is the most important thing in the whole playbook.

When you reply to a post, your reply is only seen by people who read that post. So your reply can never get much bigger than the post it sits under. If the post dies at 800 views, your reply dies with it, no matter how good it is.

Here is Rob's hit rate, where a "hit" means his reply got 100+ views:

| How many views the post ended with | Hit rate |
|---|---|
| under 2k | 1% |
| 2k to 10k | 10% |
| 10k to 100k | 24% |
| 100k+ | 37% |

20 of his 27 hits were under posts that ended above 10k views.

Timing mattered too. When Rob replied 15 to 60 minutes after the post went up, 21% of his replies were hits. Under 15 minutes it was only 4%, mostly because very fresh posts are often tiny posts that never take off. And when the author had under 1k followers, his hit rate was 3%.

So the job is simple to say and hard to do by hand. You need to find posts that are going to get big, early enough that your reply sits near the top.

## Step 1: Fix your profile first

Before you reply to anyone, fix your profile. Every good reply sends people to your profile, and your profile decides if they follow. Use your real name and a real photo of your face. Then write your bio.

Your bio is not a résumé. It's the title of a story people can follow.

People don't follow a clever one-liner. They follow a plot they can check back on. Is he still going? Did the number move? What happens next?

### Why your bio matters

Most people skim over their bio. But a bio that doesn't feel like a real, relatable person will kill your growth.

That's because your bio decides your profile conversion rate, which is the % of people who visit your profile and then follow you.

Here is mine during my first 3 days of growth:

- Day 1: 226 / 444 = 50.9%
- Day 2: 213 / 603 = 35.3%
- Day 3: 92 / 299 = 30.8%

Average: 39.4%.

Most accounts convert far lower. And when a profile converts badly, there is usually one reason, which is a bad bio.

### A good story needs two things

It needs a plot, which is something that moves so people come back. And it needs a character, someone real so people care. Your bio gives them both in one glance.

**The plot is a number that will change.** Numbers turn your account into a show, and anyone understands them in two seconds:

- Goal: $0 → $10k MRR
- 🟩⬜⬜⬜⬜⬜⬜⬜ $1.2k / $10k
- Month 4 • $840 MRR

Then update the number in your bio and post about it. Your bio is the title of the show, and your posts are the next episode.

**The character is your real life.** Don't invent a founder persona. A polished bio reads like marketing, and a specific one reads like a person. Use what actually happened: a job you left, a product you sold, years you lost, a health problem, being a parent with 60 minutes a day.

Everyone pretends to be perfect. The truth is that we all struggle, and people see themselves in that. If you don't have a painful story, don't force one. Use a real constraint instead, like time, money or starting from zero.

### The formula

[who you were] + [what you're building now] + [a number that will change]

That's past, present and future. The character comes first, then the plot.

### Why mine works

> ex-Google Maps. Sold $1.8M of software.
> Lost 6 years to depression and sleep apnea.
> Rebuilding now with Dezygn + an AI photo agency.
> Sharing all the numbers.

- **Proof:** Google Maps, $1.8M sold. This is why you should listen.
- **Wound:** Six lost years. This is why you should care.
- **Rebuild:** What I'm doing now, which is what you'll watch.
- **Promise:** Sharing all the numbers, so the plot keeps updating.

## Step 2: Day 1, reply under rising posts

On day 1, don't try to make friends yet and don't bother posting AI takes on your own account. Do what Rob did on his day 1: reply under AI posts that are about to get big.

I made about 245 replies on my Friday and gained 32 followers. Rob's day 1 was +22, so it's a similar start.

### Which posts to reply under

To know which fresh posts will get big, I ran a small tracker. I took 90 fresh AI posts and checked their views every 30 minutes for 8 hours. If a post had 300 to 1k views at 30 minutes, 44% of them went on to reach 10k. If it had 1k to 3k views at 60 minutes, 40% reached 10k. But if it still had under 1k views at 60 minutes, only 0 to 7% made it.

To be honest, 90 posts from one evening is a small sample, so treat these as rough numbers. Here is the simple version I use:

| Post age | Views right now | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| under 15 min | 300+ | Watch it and come back in a few minutes |
| under 15 min | under 300 | Skip |
| 15 to 60 min | 1k+ | Go, reply now |
| around 60 min | under 1k | Skip, it's probably not taking off |
| over 60 min | any | Skip (the finder only keeps posts under 60 min) |

And two more rules about the author:

- If the author has under 1k followers, skip. Rob's hit rate there was 3%.
- If a big account (20k+ followers) has views under 1% of its follower count, the post is flopping. Skip it.

### What a good reply looks like

A good reply is short, and it's either a sharp take or a joke. Often it has a GIF or an image.

My best reply on day 1 was "Muse is cooking : )" with an image, and it got 3,051 views. My top 3 replies all had media (3,051, 2,084 and 1,189 views).

Generic reactions don't work. Replies like "certified banger", "context?" or just an emoji got 1 to 2 views. They add nothing to the conversation, so nobody engages with them.

### The finder does this for you

Finding these posts by hand is slow. For the first part of my Friday I used a normal keyword feed, and my 115 replies got a typical 13 views each. Only 11 of them got 100+ views.

So I built a "rising posts" finder with Claude Code and treg.to. It searches X every time you hit Fetch, keeps posts under 60 minutes old that have enough views, ranks them by views per minute, and filters out flops, crypto, politics, like-pods and authors whose posts get views but no conversation.

In the evening I made 130 replies with the finder. The typical reply got 39 views instead of 13, and 35 of them got 100+ views.

One habit made the finder better over time. Every time I skipped a post, I wrote down why, and each reason became a new filter. For example, posts that were just people summoning @grok, crypto handles starting with 0x, and hockey posts about someone called Dan Muse that kept showing up when I was looking for posts about Meta's Muse.

The finder and the Chrome extension are in this kit. See the README to set them up.

## Step 3: Day 2, post your intro and pin it

On my Saturday I relaunched my intro post in Rob's format. Here it is, word for word:

> I'm 42. Founder from Belgium 🇧🇪
> Sold $1.8M of software from my email list.
> Then lost 6 years to depression and undiagnosed sleep apnea.
> Rebuilding now: $1,329 MRR + an AI photography agency.
> Looking to connect with founders and builders.
> Say hi 👋

I added a real photo and pinned it to my profile.

The format is simple. Your age and where you're from. What you did before. What went wrong. What you're building now, with a real number. What you want (to connect with founders and builders). And a clear ask at the end, which is "say hi".

Then I answered everyone who said hi. This is the part people skip, and it matters. Someone took the time to say hi to a stranger, so say hi back and make it a real reply. Answer every reply to your own posts in the first 30 to 60 minutes.

This intro post is the one Rob quote-tweeted on Sunday.

## Step 4: Post your story, not AI takes

This was a big lesson for me. Here is what my own posts did on Sunday 27 September:

| My post | Views |
|---|---|
| "For the longest time I thought money would make me happy..." | 1,575 |
| "I have already won. I have a wife and a daughter who love me. And I'm beating depression." | 1,354 |
| "JUST HIT 500 FOLLOWERS! 🥹 Friday morning I had 195. 49 hours later, 505." | 957 |
| A meme about GPT-6 Sol | 78 |

The story posts did 1,000+ views. The AI meme did 78. Rob saw the same thing on his day 1, where his AI news quote tweets flopped at around 150 views.

So here is how I split it now. My own posts are for my story. My AI takes go in replies, under someone else's post that is already rising.

I post one story post a day at a US-friendly time, which for me in Brussels is between 14:00 and 20:00.

## Step 5: Milestones that quote each other

Rob posted a milestone every 2 to 3 hours on his day 2, and they did well (1,766 and 1,112 views for the two I mentioned above).

I did the same, with one small trick. Each milestone quotes the previous one. When I hit 600, I posted "600 already 😭" as a quote of my 500 post.

That way anyone who sees the new milestone can tap through and see the last one, and the story builds on itself. It fits the bio idea from Step 1. The number is the plot, and each milestone is the next episode.

## Step 6: Connect threads and your own followers

Reach gets people to your profile. Relationships are what make them stay and talk to you. After day 1 I added three tabs to the finder:

1. **Agents & vibe coding.** A topic tab for posts about AI agents and vibe coding.
2. **Followers feed.** Posts from people who follow me, so I reply to them. If someone followed you, show up in their replies too.
3. **Let's connect.** It looks at the top "let's connect", "say hi" and "what are you building" threads, and pulls the fresh commenters from the last 60 minutes who are real builders. It keeps people with 100 to 20k followers, accounts at least 60 days old, a builder bio, no crypto or politics, no bots, and a real comment (at least 4 words, not just "congrats").

I don't know yet how well the connect tab turns into follows and real friends. I haven't measured it, so I can't give you a number for it.

## The daily routine

This is where I landed after the first few days:

- **About 150 replies a day.** About 2/3 go under rising posts, for reach. About 1/3 go to my followers and to connect threads, for relationships.
- **1 story post a day** at a US-friendly time (14:00 to 20:00 Brussels for me).
- **Milestone posts** as quotes of the previous milestone.
- **Answer every reply** to your own posts in the first 30 to 60 minutes.

## What didn't work

**AI takes as my own posts.** My GPT-6 Sol meme got 78 views, while my story posts the same day got over 1,000.

**Generic replies.** "certified banger", "context?" and emoji-only replies got 1 to 2 views each. They're a waste of a reply.

**Replying to tiny posts before I had the finder.** In my first 115 replies of Friday I replied under a lot of posts that never took off. Replies under posts that ended below 2k views averaged 5 views. Under 2k to 10k posts it was 11, and under 10k+ posts it was 30. Replies under small founder and indie posts got about 2 views, because those posts were tiny. I like those people, and they're great for relationships later, but they don't give you reach on day 1.
